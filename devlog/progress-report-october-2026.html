Remnants: where the game stands

The game has a name. Here's everything built since the last entry, and what comes next.

James Hughes · October 23, 2026 · 4 min read

First, the name: the game is called Remnants. It's a multiplayer survival and base-building game where you gather from the world, build a base that has to hold up on its own, and protect what you've got.

The last few entries were about art. Behind them, most of the past months went into systems, and the game has grown a lot. Here's where it stands.

Building

Building is the heart of Remnants and the most developed part of the game. Foundations, walls, floors, stairs and roofs snap together, and roof corners fill themselves in so a roofline reads as one clean shape. Foundations can be raised or lowered half a wall's height to deal with uneven ground.

Every piece is part of a support graph. Build too far out without support and it won't stand, and knock out the wrong wall and what's above it comes down. Pieces upgrade through five tiers, thatch, wood, stone, metal and steel, and each wall has a weak side and a strong side, which will matter a lot once raiding exists.

Holding territory

A Settlement Registry claims the land around your base. Stock it with materials and your base is maintained. Let it run dry and the base slowly decays back into the world. Doors take key locks or code locks, and chests, furnaces and other deployables can be placed and picked back up with the mallet.

Gathering and smelting

Trees and ore nodes break down in visible stages as you work them. Each node has a sweet spot that rewards accurate swings, and every tool gathers at its own rate. Furnaces turn ore into ingots, with charcoal as a by-product. Hits throw material-specific effects, so stone sparks and wood splinters.

Surviving

Hunger and thirst drain as you move, and you refill them with food, water sources, foraging and medicine. When you die you drop a bag holding your gear. When you log off, your character stays in the world asleep, and anyone can loot it, so where you log off matters.

Players can form teams of up to eight with their own chat. Sprint, crouch, free look and a first-person view that shows your own body make moving around feel grounded. The old torch has been replaced by a jar of live fireflies, which suits the world much better.

Characters and clothing

Players now have a custom character with recolorable hair, and the first clothing set: a baseball cap, hoodie, utility pants and military boots. Clothing uses layers, so items that cover the same part of the body can't be worn together.

What's next

The core loop still has a gap in the middle: you can gather and you can build, but you can't craft yet. Closing that is the next job. After that comes saving progress, then combat and raiding, which is where all the building tiers finally get tested.

The full plan is on the game page. Remnants is in closed playtests for now, and I'll keep posting here as each phase comes together, with more renders from the game itself.
